Planning page

Five ideas for a first project.

A project is easier to finish when the reader is a real person and the four pages are named before the first line of code. These are five small ideas that pass that test.

The shortlist

One reader, four pages, one smallest version

01

Dorm Kitchen

A recipe site for students who have a kettle, a microwave and no oven.

Who it is for
First-year students in campus housing.
Four pages
Home · Recipes · Plan a week · About
Smallest worth showing
Six recipes and one shopping list, no accounts.
What must be good
The recipe cards and the printable shopping list.

02

Night Bus

A late-night bus guide for students who miss the last campus shuttle.

Who it is for
Students living off campus without a car.
Four pages
Home · Routes · Stops · About
Smallest worth showing
Three routes with times, stops and a phone-sized map.
What must be good
The timetable, readable at night on a phone.

03

Club Noticeboard

A static noticeboard for a student club that is tired of lost messages in group chats.

Who it is for
A club of 20–50 members and its new joiners.
Four pages
Home · Events · Join · Archive
Smallest worth showing
One semester of events and a join page.
What must be good
The event list and the “what happens next” block.

04

Hainan Field Notes

A photo and note guide to the plants, birds and weather of the campus.

Who it is for
New students and visitors who walk around campus.
Four pages
Home · Species · Map · About
Smallest worth showing
Ten species, ten photos, one simple map.
What must be good
The photographs and the field-note captions.

05

Study Timer

A small Pomodoro timer with a week of study notes, kept in the browser.

Who it is for
Students who study in short blocks and lose track.
Four pages
Home · Timer · Log · About
Smallest worth showing
One 25-minute timer and a seven-day log.
What must be good
The timer’s start, pause and reset states.

Before you choose

Pick the idea with the smallest version you can finish, not the one with the biggest idea. A four-page site done well beats a twelve-page site half-built.
